# Urlaki: the owner's design (2026-11-01)

The owner's lore, kept close to how it was given. Lines marked *(suggestion)* are Claude's, not yet agreed.
Open questions are at the end.

## Yuat: the primordial stage

- Acts of evil feed a dimension. At first it is primordial and miasmatic, and it is called **Yuat**.
- Yuat can only be seen after drinking **Owum**: a potion of bonemeal, gold dust and emerald grit. The dust and grit
  are ground with Create's machines (our own items, milled by Create; a fallback recipe for packs without Create).
- Under Owum the world goes very dark, except for the spirits of sin, the **Onox**. They float in the air between
  folk who have been **gambling, killing or trading**.
- Onox come in three colours, one per sin: **red = hate, green = greed, blue = ambition**.
- Onox can be caught in glass bottles. A bottled Onox thrown into a campfire is destroyed, which purifies the soul.
- Drinking a bottled Onox shows a vision of Yuat: the world swirls, and shadows take that Onox's colour.

## The rite of three deaths (Yuat into Dubalba)

- Drink all three Onox and die three ways:
  1. Drink green, die by falling.
  2. Drink blue, drown.
  3. Drink red, burn.
- Each death must happen while that Onox's buff is on, and only at night, while the virtuous light gods, the
  **Alu**, sleep.
- Each Onox makes the player invulnerable to its own death while the buff lasts. A correct death turns the buff into
  a Pu buff: **Pu Vuz (green), Pu Loz (blue), Pu Song (red)**.
- If the rite isn't finished by morning, the player dies, and the angered Alu burn the player in sunlight for 30 days.
- When the rite is complete, the world goes black as if sleeping, and the player enters the mid-stage between Yuat and
  Dubalba: dark swirling, shimmering blue, red and green. A child of a random race floats there.
  - With a **golden dagger** the player can strike and kill the child. Doing so grants the egg **Bevet**.
  - If the player doesn't do it within a minute, they wake in their bed (or at their spawn) with no curse.
- **Bevet** lights a portal of **gilded blackstone**, the way flint and steel lights a Nether portal. The portal
  leads to Dubalba.

## Dubalba

- A giant grid of blackstone variants: hallways running in random directions forever, with chambers holding the
  souls of sin.
- A soul touches the player and vanishes. Nothing seems to happen, but the player now carries an **invisible curse**
  and a **visible buff**.
- The more souls that touch the player, the likelier the player is to cause evil in the world.
- The buffs are temporary: better trade prices (green), more damage dealt (red), swim speed and water breathing
  (blue). They last longer as more sins are collected, with diminishing returns.
- Dubalba is also where cured Urlaki spirits are banished: a tormented afterlife.

## Urlaki: the affliction

- It comes from trading or other dealings with folk while cursed.
  - Some folk refuse to trade with the player; some threaten the player to leave.
  - If folk attack the player, guards arrest or kill them, but the player is also asked to leave to keep the peace.
- Folk killed by a cursed player have their souls bound to the player (held in thrall), and the body becomes an
  Urlaki version of itself.
- **Thralled souls are spent on dark powers:**
  - Red: stored and used for accumulated damage.
  - Green: force trades through fear, at reduced prices.
  - Blue: ambition (to be decided; ideas below).

## Urlaki folk

- They hide among ordinary folk until they have overthrown a whole town. They are nocturnal, and their skin is
  slightly paler; it's hard to tell.
- They spread by swallowing sleeping folk whole. While feeding, an Urlaki becomes a giant version of its own head
  with bulging, horrific eyes (owner's reference image: a huge grotesque head engulfing a sleeper's upper body).
  They feed only while others sleep, because the giant-head form is vulnerable.
- **Hunters:** villagers who discover Urlaki among them hire Urlaki hunters from out of town. After some time the
  hunters arrive and try to kill the Urlaki while they're vulnerable.
  - Urlaki must be splashed with milk and attacked with iron weapons.
  - In their normal forms they can be captured, then imprisoned or put in vats for the pink slime treatment.

## The cure: pink slime

- The afflicted is put in a chamber and covered in pink slime. The slime forms a pupal state around them; they can
  walk around covered in it, seeing through light-emitting eyes.
- After a time the slime leaves the host and two beings result: the restored person and a new slime being. The
  Urlaki spirit is banished to Dubalba.
  - Example: a cat folk becomes a Cat-Urlaki, is captured and treated, and comes out as the restored cat plus a slime
    being.
- The chamber is built from plans drawn up by a tinkerer rat and two other characters (races to be decided).
- Tinkerer rats should tie into Create.

## Later: Ulm

- Under the right conditions, Urlaki spirits break through to the overworld and cause chaos beyond turning folk.
- Over time they organise Dubalba into a labyrinth, evolving it into **Ulm**: a hellish village of sins made flesh.
- Getting into Ulm requires committing acts of great evil and committing to evil magic.

## The owner's answers (2026-11-01, later)

- **Deaths in the rite:** the matching death strikes and is absorbed by the Onox. The player lives, and the buff
  becomes the Pu buff.
- **Blue souls:** *Usurp* (agreed): spend blue souls to secretly take a town's top seat. The town pays tribute and
  its guards look away.
- **The vision child:** a ghostly spirit that dissolves into the three colours when struck (agreed).
- **The player is the Balba, Quoin of the Urlaki** (a kind of head vampire). The magic number is 3.
  - The player doesn't eat the unafflicted. Instead the Balba becomes a giant head, 4x4x4 blocks, and scoops up 3
    afflicted Urlaki in its mouth to carry them to another town and spread the curse there.
  - The head power comes only once an entire town has been afflicted, and works only at night or in a thunderstorm.
- **Cleansing the player** is the reverse of becoming the Balba:
  - atone for every sin with acts of great charity and virtue;
  - cure every Urlaki with the slime method (capture, vat, split);
  - account for every Urlaki destroyed and condemned to Dubalba. To free their souls, the player enters a special
    vat, is covered in slime, travels to Dubalba, turns into the giant head and swallows the condemned souls.
- **The Gate of the Balba (when Dubalba has become Ulm):** the player is still the Quoin, but now there is a gate.
  - Picture an arch: the Urlaki are its stones and the Balba is its keystone. Once the arch is large enough, a Gate
    fits it.
  - The Gate is a giant three-eyed head (red, green and blue eyes) with a black hole for a mouth.
  - **The Fourth Death** collapses Ulm back into Dubalba. To commit it, the player must truly die, which resets the
    overworld back to its original state.
    *(Claude: build "original state" as undoing everything the Urlaki did (towns restored, curses lifted, Ulm closed),
    not regenerating terrain. Confirm.)*

## Dog City and the Urlaki (owner)

- The Whipped is the metaphorical keystone of Dog City's culture. The dogs were wild, and through the pain of the
  lash they evolved. It is the unpleasant ballast to their peace and harmony. Lose the Whipped and they lose their
  history; the balance breaks and they become wild dogs again (hence wolves).
- The link to the Urlaki is the sin that gained the balance. The dogs don't seek to surpass the equilibrium they
  have; they aren't greedy, don't hold ambition as a virtue, and don't kill out of hate or revenge. But their society
  was forged by those who do. They are man and dog.
- The Urlaki are devious by nature and would most likely turn the dogs' adherence to tradition against them.

## Claude's suggestions (not agreed)

- **Blue souls (ambition)** (Usurp was chosen):
  - *Usurp*: spend blue souls to secretly take a town's top seat. The town pays you tribute and its guards look away.
    This fits the Urlaki goal of overthrowing whole towns.
  - *Command*: wake a thralled Urlaki as a servant that follows and fights for you, up to a limit.
  - *Reach*: surface from any water at a gilded blackstone portal you've lit, a kind of drowned travel. It fits
    blue's water theme (drowning, water breathing).
- **Plans for the slime chamber:** a tinkerer rat, a frog alchemist (frog towns already have a potion shop) and an
  Ithk (eyeball folk; the "light-emitting eyes" of the pupal state echo them).
- **Slime beings could become a new race**, born only from cures, so every cure adds someone to the world.
- **Hunters arrive as inn guests**, which ties the hunters into the inn and visitor system.
- **Possible lore tie: The Whipped**, who "kneels so that we may stand", may bear Dog City's sins and keep the Urlaki
  out. Killing him lets the curse in, and the dogs turn into wolves.
- **The vision child:** present it as a ghostly vision rather than flesh; when struck, it dissolves into the three
  colours.

2. **Players as Urlaki.** Does a player ever become an Urlaki folk-style (giant head, feeding), or does the player
   stay the cursed one who makes Urlaki?
3. **Can a cursed player be cured or cleansed?** Burning Onox at a campfire purifies; does it also lift the Dubalba
   curse?
4. **The two other races for the slime chamber plans.**
